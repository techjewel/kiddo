# Photo credits

Every photograph here was cropped to a square and resized to 512px;
nothing else was changed. The crop is centred except on `fish.jpg`,
where centring cut the snout off — that one is offset to keep the whole
fish in frame. Licences below are the originals.

The fruits, the animals and most of the birds are each the lead image of
the matching English Wikipedia article, which makes them vetted,
representative pictures of the subject. The household things, the
flowers and four of the birds are not: an encyclopaedia illustrates
"chair" with a Chippendale, "book" with a Gutenberg Bible, "daisy" with
two small ones on a mossy rock and "peafowl" with a peacock that isn't
fanned, which teaches a three-year-old nothing. Those were picked by
hand from Wikimedia Commons for one rule — one ordinary example of the
thing, big in the frame.

"Fish" is the same problem: the lead image of *Fish* is one long barb
filling a wide frame, and no square crop of it holds a whole fish. It
uses the lead image of *Goldfish* instead — a fish a child has actually
seen, whole, head to tail.

## Jobs

The people are not from Wikipedia. An encyclopaedia illustrates
"physician" with a portrait of a Victorian surgeon and "mail carrier"
with a 1969 retirement photograph, neither of which is a picture of a
job to a three-year-old. These are contemporary photographs found
through Openverse and Wikimedia Commons under the same one rule as the
household things: one ordinary person doing the job, big in the frame,
wearing whatever it is the job wears — the coat, the helmet, the toque,
the hard hat.

Two of the jobs he asked about are not here. Nothing free and current
showed a postman or a tailor clearly enough to be worth a tile, and a
photograph a child has to have explained to him teaches him nothing.
